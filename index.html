Growth

Additional Analysis
Warning

June saw 238 clicks vs. 277 in May — first month-over-month decline since tracking began. Sessions are growing but engagement is softening. The quality-vs-quantity tension from May is now confirmed as a trend.

Stabilized

Workshop 101 flat at 164 sessions

After the sharp May decline (570 → 199 → 159), June held steady at 164. It's no longer bleeding traffic, but it's also not recovering. The post-email-deprecation baseline appears to be ~160 sessions/month.

Consistent

Salesforce in Slack remains the quality leader

SF Channels Implementation Guide grew to 264 sessions in June (vs. 246 in May). Category maintains strong clicks and manageable bounce rates. This is the engagement profile every other category should aim for.

Shift

Use Cases growing but engagement mixed

Category sessions climbed across the board — Internal Comms (121), Legal (97), Execs (72), HR (69), Marketing (57). But bounce rates are rising alongside traffic. Volume is up; depth is questionable.

Recommendations
1

Investigate the click decline and bounce spike

June is the first month where sessions grew but clicks dropped. Run a GA4 cohort analysis: are returning visitors clicking less, or are new visitors (who bounce more) diluting engagement? If it's the latter, traffic quality is the issue — not content.

2

Identify and segment the June referral traffic source

Referral traffic jumped 374% in June (70→332 sessions). Pull the referring hostname breakdown for June — if it's a specific campaign or partner link, assess whether that traffic source aligns with Hub goals or should be redirected to more appropriate landing pages.

3

Expand Salesforce in Slack content

SF Channels Implementation Guide grew 7% MoM and maintains the best engagement profile on the site. A troubleshooting guide, video walkthrough, or "Common Mistakes" resource could capture more of this high-intent admin audience without relying on external traffic sources.

4

Test internal cross-promotion to stabilize clicks

Clicks are declining as traffic grows — visitors aren't exploring beyond their landing page. Add a "Related Resources" module or in-content CTAs to high-traffic, low-click articles (e.g., Slackbot Admin Toolkit) to encourage deeper engagement.

About the Slack Success Hub

The Success Hub is Slack's primary post-sale, self-serve content destination for admins — the place we send customers after they sign to help them launch, adopt, and get more out of Slack. It lives at slack.com/resources and is built on WordPress. Traffic has grown 50% YTD in CY 2026.

How to Read This Report

Sessions

A visit to the site. One session can include multiple pageviews. Primary volume metric — how many admins are coming to the Hub.

Pageviews

Total pages loaded. A ratio above 1.1 pageviews/session means visitors are exploring beyond their landing page — a positive signal.

Clicks

Outbound clicks to Slack resources or product pages. Our engagement quality signal — a click means content prompted a next step. We want this growing faster than sessions.

Bounce (GA4)

In GA4, a bounce = a session under 10 seconds with no interaction. Someone reading an article for 3 minutes does NOT bounce. High bounce = wrong audience, not bored reader.

What Good Looks Like

✅ Healthy signals

  • Clicks growing faster than sessions — content quality improving, not just volume
  • Returning visitor % above 60% — admins coming back (currently 61–67% monthly)
  • Low bounce on high-intent categories — Salesforce in Slack consistently under 35%
  • Category distribution balanced — no single category dominating to an unhealthy degree

⚠️ Watch signals

  • Bounce spike on a single article — usually a traffic source mismatch, not a content problem
  • Category traffic dropping 2+ months in a row — content may be aging out
  • Clicks flat while sessions grow — traffic without engagement
  • New pages with near-zero sessions — distribution may not be connected to publishing
